gigapair

A G whose bar is a block of data. Gigapair moves files as blocks, and the one in the mark is the one that matters: yellow, arriving.

The mark

The initial, drawn with the app's own shapes. Gigapair moves files as 4 MiB blocks, each checked with BLAKE3 before it counts; the bar of the G ends in one of those blocks, and the progress strip in the app is made of the same squares.

  • The letterA G on a rounded square, one stroke of even weight. The top arm stops before the corner, so the mouth stays open and the letter reads as G, not as a square ring, from 16 px to a poster.
  • The blockThe bar runs in from the right at mid height and ends in a yellow block, joined to the black along one straight edge. Yellow is the only colour in the mark, as in the app, where it always means data or the action that moves it.
  • The constructionOne centreline (dashed): a 36-unit square with radius-10 corners and a bar at its centre, stroked at 11. Everything is on the 64-unit grid, so it renders crisp at 16, 32 and 64 px.

App icon and sizes

Graphite tile in both themes, so the icon reads on a light or dark taskbar.

Tray: idle and busy

No tile in the tray, just the G. Idle, the block is hollow: nothing is moving. Busy, it fills with yellow. One glance, no badge.

Colour and type

Neutral ink and graphite carry the interface; signal yellow is reserved for data and the action that moves it. Status is told by shape first (a solid or dashed link, a filled or hollow cell) and colour second, so it survives colour blindness and both themes.

One family, and width is its voice: amounts of data are set wide, because they are the big thing on screen; everything else stays at normal width. Numbers use tabular figures so speeds and sizes don't jitter while they count.

The window

The same screen as the current app, with every control it has today, redrawn. Shown at the real window size (1040 × 700). Move the progress, cut the connection, switch to relay, or drag any file over the drop zone.

When you cut the connection, only the in-flight cell is lost, which is exactly what Gigapair does today.

What changes

The app today, with the same data. It works, but it looks like any gpui-component demo: no colour of its own, and a few hierarchy problems that make it harder to use.
